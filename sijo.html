Sijo

A Korean verse form related to haiku and tanka, and has a lot of and comprised of three lines of 14-16 syllables each, for a total of 44-46 syllables. Each line contains a pause near the middle, though the break need not be metrical. The first half of the line contains six to nine syllables; the second half should contain no fewer than five. Whatever the subject, the first line introduces an idea or story, the second supplies a “turn,” and the third provides closure. Modern sijo are sometimes printed in six lines.
